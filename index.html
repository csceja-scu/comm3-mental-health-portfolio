The catalyst

Three lives lost.
One campus forced to respond.

My work began with a hard question: how should a student tell the story of a campus mental-health crisis without reducing it to statistics or spectacle?

For this COMM 3 portfolio, I connected research, an interview with former SCU journalism senior lecturer Barbara Kelley, and visual storytelling to document how grief, academic pressure, and student advocacy shaped the university's response.

Perspective

As an SCU student, veteran, first-generation Mexican American, and tech worker, I approached this project with attention to pressure and systems: the personal pressure students carry, and the institutional systems responsible for meeting students before crisis.

  1. Fall 2021

    Three student deaths affect the SCU community. The deaths, including two suicides, left students grieving and looking for answers.

  2. Afterward

    Students organized and demanded stronger mental-health resources. The movement focused attention on Counseling and Psychological Services, known as CAPS.

  3. Response

    SCU allocated millions to improve CAPS. The response became part of a larger conversation about care, access, and the pressure students face.

The evidence

The hidden weight of a generation.

While these deaths were individual tragedies, their impact was amplified by larger patterns in higher education: distress, academic pressure, and a gap between need and support-seeking. To frame the problem, this portfolio references a 2021 study of 23,307 higher-education students one year into the COVID-19 pandemic.

55.1%

Source: Schmits et al. (2021), Psychological Distress among Students in Higher Education.

Visual story

Place, pressure, and memory.

The original visual project by Carlos Ceja and Charlie Gray used campus and nearby locations to help viewers imagine and reflect on what happened. This web version presents key moments natively, with the full PDF preserved as the archive artifact.

Artist statement

In this visual piece, we intend to represent the impact of these deaths through the scenes where they had impact or occurred — allowing our audience to imagine and reflect on these events. As SCU students creating this story, we empathize with the struggles these Broncos faced. We hope to represent this story thoughtfully through the eyes of fellow Broncos, for Broncos.
